# E26 image prompts (the two sages who fought as birds)

Each prompt below is only the **[SCENE]** part: the style prefix from [image-style.md](image-style.md) is added automatically. Recurring characters use their description lines word for word from [characters.md](characters.md): Vasishtha (with his E03 add-on) is always on the LEFT and Vishvamitra (Stage 2) always on the RIGHT, and the same for the Adi bird (Vasishtha) on the LEFT and the Baka heron (Vishvamitra) on the RIGHT; the transformation is shown only as "the sage's outline dissolving into feathers", never a half-man, half-bird body. Harishchandra uses the E20 crowned line, the E24 "after giving away his kingdom" line and the E25 cremation-ground servant line; Brahma, the devas, Shabala, Trishanku, Nimi, Dasharatha, the boy Rama (shown without Lakshmana, so there is exactly one bow), Bahuka in exile, the pregnant young queen, the townspeople and the places use their sheet lines. Characters and places that appear only here keep one fixed line, repeated word for word: the river where Vasishtha does tapas (`a wide sacred river at dawn, calm grey-gold water, soft mist, sandy banks with tall kusha grass, no buildings, no boats`), the ordinary heron (`a natural white heron of normal size, standing on one leg in shallow water at a reed-lined river edge`), the false ascetic of the "baka-vrati" verse (`a false ascetic, a lean man about forty-five, light-brown skin, shaven head with a small tuft, clean-shaven, no beard, no moustache, spotless white silk dhoti and shawl, three gold rings on his left hand, a rudraksha rosary in his right hand, exactly two arms`), the villagers caught in the war (`villagers in plain undyed cotton dhotis and simple cotton sarees, faces turned away, the far figures lost in dust and rain`) and the palm-leaf book (`a palm-leaf pothi bundle of long narrow leaves tied with a cord between wooden covers`). The fish is always small and far below the surface; no animal is ever caught or harmed. Every prompt follows [picture-rules.md](picture-rules.md). The source of this episode is the Bhagavata 9.7 and the Markandeya Purana, chapter 9. 47 pictures, each shown from its **starts at** words until the next picture's words.
