# E31 image prompts (Kapila: the sixty thousand princes call him a thief)

Each prompt below is only the **[SCENE]** part: the style prefix from [image-style.md](image-style.md) is added automatically. Recurring characters use their description lines word for word from [characters.md](characters.md): Kapila (always with the god phrase, "exactly two arms" and a job for each hand), Devahuti (two teaching pictures, with Kapila's "with his mother" add-on), the sixty thousand sons of Sagara (never counted, with the digging and charging add-ons; the burning uses Kapila's Bhagavata add-on, flames from the princes' own bodies, no fire near Kapila, no fire from his eyes), the sacrificial white horse (with the "tied by a loose rope" add-on beside Kapila), Indra (the stealing add-on in the recap), Lord Vishnu (one faint image behind Kapila at "avatar"), King Sagara, Prince Asamanjasa (the leaving and Sarayu add-ons from E32), the townspeople, Ganga as a river of light (the Patala add-on), and the places Patala, the ash mound of Patala, the great chasm, the ancient Ganga Sagar, the Ayodhya terrace and gates and the Sarayu bank. Kapila's eyes are "clearly closed" as characters.md asks, except in the burning (picture 23, where the script's own screen note reads "कपिल की आँखें खुलती हुईं"), where this script says he opened them ("कपिल ने केवल आँखें खोलीं"), in the two teaching pictures with Devahuti (16, 41, a flashback where closed eyes while facing his mother would read as blindness), and in the pictures after the burning that the script describes with his compassionate eyes; there he has "eyes open, gentle compassionate face", the E33 wording, never with fire or rays from his eyes. Where Patala shares a picture with long character lines it keeps one short fixed form (`the dark cavern of Patala, rough rock walls, a dim shaft of light from far above`) so the prompt stays under the length limit. Characters and places that appear only here keep one fixed line, repeated word for word: the village storyteller from E27 (`an old village storyteller, about seventy, thin, dark-brown skin, short white beard, short white hair, bare head, plain white dhoti and a brown shawl, exactly two arms`), the Kapil Muni temple of today (`a small ancient temple of Kapila on a sandy island shore, whitewashed walls, a low curved spire, a saffron flag on a bamboo pole, palm trees, no modern buildings, no electric lights, no vehicles`), the pilgrims (`pilgrims in plain cotton dhotis and simple cotton sarees of muted ochre, green and blue, the far figures fading into mist beyond counting`) and the old pilgrim of the proverb (`an old pilgrim, about seventy, thin, dark-brown skin, long white beard, white hair, plain white dhoti and cloth wrap, exactly two arms`). Nobody is shown wounded, weapons never touch anyone, there are no bodies or bones in the ash, and every fire stays in a lamp or in the princes' own burning. Valmiki's version (Kapila's angry roar) is shown only as two pothi bundles and a storyteller, never as an angry Kapila. Every prompt follows [picture-rules.md](picture-rules.md). The source of this episode is the Bhagavata (9.8, 3.24-33) with Valmiki (Bala Kanda) for comparison. 50 pictures, each shown from its **starts at** words until the next picture's words. Picture 1 starts at "उन्हें अपना खोया घोड़ा" because the very first words, "साठ हज़ार राजकुमार", recur in later lines; assemble.py moves the first picture to 0:00, so it also covers those opening words.
