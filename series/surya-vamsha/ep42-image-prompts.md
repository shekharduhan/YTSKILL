# E42 image prompts (Who was Raghu? The Bhagavata gives him one line)

Each prompt below is only the **[SCENE]** part: the style prefix from [image-style.md](image-style.md) is added automatically. Recurring characters use their description lines word for word from [characters.md](characters.md) (Shri Ram; King Raghu, and Prince Raghu facing Indra on Airavata for the famous battle; Kalidasa; Parikshit and Shukadeva; Trishanku cursed for the memory picture; Vasishtha at Mithila and at his hermitage; Kalidasa's Dilipa as king and serving Nandini, and Dilipa son of Anshuman for the "two Dilipas" picture; Dasharatha and Kaikeyi; Harishchandra; Ikshvaku and the young Vikukshi; the townspeople, the courtiers, the lion and the recurring places). Things that appear only here keep one fixed line each: the newborn Rama (`the newborn Rama, a radiant baby boy with soft blue skin wrapped in yellow silk cloth, a little black hair, round peaceful face, eyes open, a soft golden glow around him, no jewellery`); the poet-saint Tulsidas (`the poet-saint Tulsidas, an elderly man about seventy, wheat-brown skin, clean-shaven, no beard, no moustache, shaven head with a small white tuft, sandal-paste tilak, plain white cotton dhoti and white shawl, tulsi bead garland, exactly two arms, writing with a reed pen on loose handmade paper leaves by a small clay oil lamp`); the two scholars of the "two readings" picture, on the left (`an elderly scholar pandit, about sixty, wheat-brown skin, clean-shaven, no beard, no moustache, shaven head with a small tuft, sandal-paste tilak, white cotton dhoti and white shawl, sacred thread, exactly two arms`) and on the right (`a younger scholar pandit, about thirty-five, light-brown skin, short neat black beard, black hair tied in a knot, saffron tilak, cream cotton dhoti and pale blue shawl, sacred thread, exactly two arms`); and the anonymous figure of the reflections, as in E33 and E41 (`a lone small human figure in plain white cloth, exactly two arms, seen from behind`). The single line of the Bhagavata is always one long palm leaf with a glowing line, never readable; lineage cards are the sun-disc lineage tree without writing; Dirghabahu, Aja and Indumati are never drawn as people, so no one-off king can be mistaken for them. The lion never touches the cow or the king, and Indra's vajra is never thrown. Every prompt follows [picture-rules.md](picture-rules.md). The sources of this episode are the Bhagavata (9.10), Kalidasa's Raghuvamsha, Valmiki's Bala Kanda and the Ramcharitmanas. 46 pictures, each shown from its **starts at** words until the next picture's words.
