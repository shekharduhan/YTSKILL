# E41 image prompts (Khatvanga: forty-eight minutes to live)

Each prompt below is only the **[SCENE]** part: the style prefix from [image-style.md](image-style.md) is added automatically. Recurring characters use their description lines word for word from [characters.md](characters.md) (Khatvanga with the "last muhurta" and "dissolving" add-ons when he meditates; Mulaka with the palace women; Parashurama; Indra, the devas and the asuras; Kakutstha on the bull and Muchukunda for the memory pictures; Vishnu as Vasudeva; Parikshit and Shukadeva). Things that appear only here keep one fixed line each: the boy Mulaka alone (`the boy Mulaka, a small boy about five, wheat-brown skin, short black hair, plain white dhoti, no crown, no jewellery, exactly two arms`); the clock of the muhurta, shown as the period water clock instead of a sand-glass (`an ancient Indian water clock (ghati): a small copper bowl with a tiny hole in its base floating in a wide clay vessel of clear water, the bowl slowly filling and sinking`); the gods' flying chariot (`a small golden aerial chariot (vimana) shaped like a temple shrine, a single dome on carved pillars, floating on white clouds, no wings, no propellers, no wheels`); the room of Khatvanga's last muhurta (`a quiet palace shrine room in Ayodhya, carved sandstone pillars, a small stone altar with a golden sun-disc and exactly one clay oil lamp, no other fire`); the family priest of the "today" picture (`an elderly family priest, about sixty-five, wheat-brown skin, clean-shaven, no beard, no moustache, shaven head with a small tuft, sandal-paste tilak, white cotton dhoti and white shawl, sacred thread, exactly two arms`) and the couple who consult him (`a young couple, the man in a cream cotton dhoti and cloth wrap, clean-shaven, the woman in a simple red cotton saree with her head covered, both with hands joined`); and the anonymous figure of the reflections, as in E33 (`a lone small human figure in plain white cloth, exactly two arms, seen from behind`). The reflection lines (सूत्र-पंक्ति) are empty symbolic scenes with at most one tiny figure; text cards and lineage cards are the sun-disc lineage tree without writing; the earlier Dasharatha is never drawn, so he cannot be mistaken for Rama's father. Nobody is wounded, Parashurama's axe touches no one, and the serpent Takshaka is only a far shadow. Every prompt follows [picture-rules.md](picture-rules.md). The source of this episode is the Bhagavata (9.9 and 2.1). 48 pictures, each shown from its **starts at** words until the next picture's words.
