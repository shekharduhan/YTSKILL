# E22 image prompts (Rohita's six years in the forest: charaiveti, keep walking)

Each prompt below is only the **[SCENE]** part: the style prefix from [image-style.md](image-style.md) is added automatically. Recurring characters use their description lines word for word from [characters.md](characters.md): Rohita uses the forest line all episode, with the add-on "a little older, about twenty-one" only in the sixth-year pictures (Indra watching over him, arriving at the hut, the thought, the offer); the Indra-is-a-friend and "first spoken" pictures look back at the young runaway and use the plain line; where a far-off figure stands for him (a tiny traveller seen from high above) no line is pasted; the recap uses the fifteen line (leaving Ayodhya) and Harishchandra in middle age with the E21 dropsy add-on (crown off on a stand, so only one crown is drawn); Varuna at the palace door uses the human-height line as in E21; Indra appears as the old brahmin traveller whenever he blocks or teaches Rohita, and in his own form only after the narration names him (and never revealed to Rohita face to face: the "I am with you" picture uses the brahmin disguise, the "friend" picture shows him unseen beside Rohita); Ajigarta, his two other sons and Shunahshepa use their E23 lines (the hut picture shows the three sons, not the mother). One-off looks, fixed word for word: the messenger who brings the news is `a wandering forest traveller, a stocky man about forty-five, dark-brown skin, short black beard, grey cloth turban, plain brown cotton dhoti and shawl, a cloth bundle on a stick over his left shoulder, exactly two arms`; the destiny that sits and walks with Rohita in the shloka pictures is `a soft translucent golden shadow of his own shape, featureless, no face`. In the busiest two- and three-person pictures the road and hut lines are cut to their first words (`a dusty forest road under tall sal trees, no buildings`, `a poor thatched hut at the edge of a dry forest, a single empty clay pot, harsh dusty light`, or `outside a poor thatched hut`) to stay under the 160-word limit; the character lines are never cut. The Sanskrit verse cards, labels and the "चरैवेति" lettering are added in the edit, never drawn. Every prompt follows [picture-rules.md](picture-rules.md). 48 pictures, each shown from its **starts at** words until the next picture's words.
