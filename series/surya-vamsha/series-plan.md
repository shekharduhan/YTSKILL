# The Sun Dynasty: Bhagavata Purana Skandha 9 (chapters 6-12)

**84 episodes of about 10 minutes, in 10 arcs**, one arc for each king on your list, told in the order the
Bhagavata tells them. Every title and thumbnail pairing was run through `skills/yt-package/title.py`:
the average score is 88.8/100 and the lowest is 80. The tool checks length, repetition and vagueness. It
cannot predict views.

A 10-minute episode is about **1,500 spoken words** at 150 words a minute. Hindi scripts written so far: E01-E77 (`epNN-*-hindi.md` in this folder), all in the [Hindi style guide](style-guide-hindi.md).

## Language and voice

**Hindi is the master language.** Each episode's Hindi narration script is the one you record. The
English script is a working draft. Write titles and thumbnails in Hindi for a Hindi channel; the
English titles below are the linted drafts to translate from.

**The narrator.** The scripts are written for a deep, slow, theatrical Hindi narration voice, with
pauses marked. The voice has to come from one of these:

1. **A licensed agreement with the actor you want**, made through their management. This is the
   only way to use a specific famous actor's voice.
2. **A professional Hindi voice artist** hired for this style: deep baritone, measured, dramatic
   pauses. Ask for a 60-second sample of the E02 hook before you commit.
3. **A stock Hindi text-to-speech voice** whose licence allows commercial YouTube use, set to a slow
   rate and a low pitch.

Do not clone a real person's voice without their written consent. Indian courts have granted
well-known actors personality-rights orders against AI imitations of their voice, and YouTube
requires creators to disclose realistic synthetic content.

## How the episode count was decided

Each king gets as many episodes as the sources can fill with **ten real minutes**, not padding:

- Where the Bhagavata goes into detail (Sagara, Bhagiratha, Rama), the episodes follow its chapters.
- Where the Bhagavata is brief (Trishanku gets about two verses, Raghu and Aja one line), the arc
  says so in its first episode, then continues with the texts that tell the full story: Valmiki's
  Ramayana, Kalidasa's *Raghuvamsha*, the Harivamsha, the Markandeya Purana and the Aitareya Brahmana.
- Lesser-known kings and figures from the same chapters (Kakutstha, Dhundhumara, Saubhari, Anaranya,
  Saudasa, Khatvanga, Maru) each get their own episode inside the closest arc. They fill out the
  arcs, and the stories are less well known.

Rama's arc is 22 episodes because his story is the longest. You can cut it to about 12 by merging
neighbouring episodes, or make it longer by splitting each kanda further.

## The rule this series runs on

**Name the text each detail comes from, out loud.** Your audience knows the famous versions:
Harishchandra's test of truth, Raghu's conquests, Indumati's garland, Lav and Kush fighting Rama's
army. None of those are in the Bhagavata. If you present them as Bhagavata, the comments will catch
it. If you say "the Bhagavata gives Raghu one line, and the famous story is Kalidasa's", that contrast
holds viewers.

Keep a small fixed label in one corner of the screen and change it whenever the source changes:
`SB 9.x` · `VALMIKI` · `KALIDASA` · `HARIVAMSHA` · `MARKANDEYA` · `AITAREYA` · `MAHABHARATA` · `PADMA`.

**Check every quote before you record.** The sources below are given by chapter, kanda or canto.
I left out verse numbers I was not sure of. Choose one translation of each text, show its wording
on screen, and name it in the description. The facts I am least sure of are listed in
[Check these before recording](#check-these-before-recording).

Upload on the days your own Analytics show your viewers are most active (Audience tab). Start each
arc on the same weekday so viewers learn when the next one comes. Make the last line of every episode
point to the next one by name. **When a week goes wrong, drop the Shorts first and keep the episode.**

## Check these before recording

Notes on each arc where my memory of the text may be wrong, or where versions of the story disagree:

- **E05 Dhundhumara**: the Bhagavata says 21,000 sons and three survivors. Uttanka asking for the
  demon to be killed comes from the Mahabharata, so label it.
- **E03 Nimi**: SB 9.13 is outside chapters 6-12, but Nimi is Ikshvaku's son. Check the curse sequence and the eyelid (*nimesha*) detail in your translation.
- **E09 Saubhari**: confirm how long he meditated under the water before putting any number on screen.
- **E13 Muchukunda**: confirm he is Mandhata's son in your translation. His cave story is SB 10.51,
  outside chapters 6-12. Also, the Ambarisha who was Mandhata's son is **not** the Ambarisha of the
  Durvasa story (SB 9.4-5). Viewers mix the two up, so say so in E12 or E13.
- **E14 Anaranya**: check that the Bhagavata lists him among Trishanku's ancestors. His curse on
  Ravana is told in Valmiki's Uttara Kanda.
- **E16 Trishanku's three sins**: this explanation of the name comes from the Harivamsha. Other
  texts give other reasons. Say that there are several versions.
- **E20-E26 Harishchandra** (the bird battle is E26, after the truth test, as in the Markandeya Purana): the Bhagavata names the priests at the sacrifice and says Shunahshepha
  was spared and later became Vishvamitra's son Devarata (SB 9.16). The "keep walking" verses
  (*charaiveti*) are from the Aitareya Brahmana. Only quote them as the Bhagavata if your
  translation actually contains them there.
- **E24-E25**: the truth-test story is from the Markandeya Purana (it is also in the Devi Bhagavata).
  Look up the chapter numbers before you show them.
- **E31 Kapila**: the line saying Kapila did not burn the princes, and that they burned because of
  their own offence, is the reveal of the episode. Read it word for word.
- **E37 Jahnu**: the detail is mainly Valmiki's. Check whether your Bhagavata translation mentions it
  at SB 9.15.
- **E42-E46 Raghu, E47-E49 Aja**: almost everything here is Kalidasa. The canto numbers are
  approximate, so check them. Each text gives a different family tree: in the Bhagavata, Raghu's
  father is Dirghabahu; in Kalidasa it is Dilipa; check Valmiki's list in the Bala Kanda.
- **E57 the bow**: "300 men carried it" is how I remember the Bhagavata's wording. Check it, because
  Valmiki gives a different number.
- **E74 Hanuman's prayer**: SB 5.19, outside Skandha 9. It is worth including because it is the
  Bhagavata's own picture of Rama.
- **E75**: in the Bhagavata, Rama overhears the remark himself while walking the city at night in
  disguise. In Valmiki the report reaches him through others. Name the difference.
- **E76**: the explanation of the names Kusha and Lava is Valmiki's (Uttara Kanda).
- **E79**: the battle over the horse is in later texts (the Padma Purana and the Jaimini Ashvamedha),
  not in Valmiki or the Bhagavata. Name both texts.
- **E84**: in the Bhagavata, the dynasty ends with Sumitra in the Kali age, and Maru lives on by yoga
  in the village of Kalapa. Lava's descendants are not traced in the Bhagavata.
