# E45 image prompts (Raghu's digvijaya: the conquest of the four directions)

Each prompt below is only the **[SCENE]** part: the style prefix from [image-style.md](image-style.md) is added automatically. Recurring characters use their description lines word for word from [characters.md](characters.md) (King Raghu; Prince Raghu and Indra on Airavata for the E44 recap; the poet Kalidasa, always with "the palm leaves show only faint scratched lines"; the conquered kings; King Sagara and the defeated warriors for the Sagara reminder; the townspeople of Ayodhya; Raghu after giving everything away and Kautsa for the E46 bridge; the places Ayodhya court, gates and terrace, the yajnashala and Raghu's emptied palace). On campaign Raghu's line is pasted word for word up to "exactly two arms" and the sheet's campaign add-on (`, silver armour over the crimson robe, exactly one bow`) takes the place of the closing "no bow", so the prompt never says both; each campaign prompt names the hand that holds the bow and adds "no other bow anywhere in the picture". Things that appear only here keep one fixed line each: Raghu's army (`Raghu's army, foot soldiers in silver-grey armour with spears, war elephants with plain red caparisons, horsemen, plain crimson banners with no emblems, the far ranks lost in golden dust`); the running map of the script, drawn without any labels, which are added in the edit (`an ancient hand-painted cloth map of the land of India spread on a wooden table, showing only drawn rivers, hills, forests and coastlines in ochre, green and blue, no borders, no symbols`); the anonymous figure of the reflections, as in E41 and E43 (`a lone small human figure in plain white cloth, exactly two arms, seen from behind`); the Vanga boatmen (`river warriors of Vanga in plain white dhotis on long wooden boats with curved prows, holding spears and oars, the far boats fading into river mist`); the Parasikas (`Parasika horsemen of Kalidasa's poem, riders with long dark beards in long belted tunics of dark red and brown, tall felt caps, on tall slender horses, spears held upright, the far riders lost in desert dust`); the Hunas (`northern horsemen in fur-trimmed leather coats and round fur caps, on sturdy shaggy horses, the far riders lost in dust`); the Kamboja gift horses (`a line of tall fine horses with glossy coats and plain red halters, no riders, the far horses fading into dust`); the mountain clans (`mountain clansmen in thick undyed woollen wraps, their long knives laid down on the ground before them, no bows, the far ones fading into mist`); and the horse caravan (`a caravan of horse traders in long belted tunics leading tall slender horses, the far horses fading into dust`). The kings of Kalinga and Pragjyotisha and the Kamboja chieftain appear once each, described in their pictures. As the script asks, the peoples are Kalidasa's names only: no modern regional dress, flags or features. Armies never touch: a stretch of ground or water stays between them, no bodies, no blood. The conquered kings always keep or receive their crowns (dharma-vijaya). Every prompt follows [picture-rules.md](picture-rules.md). The source of this episode is Kalidasa, Raghuvamsha (canto 4), not the Bhagavata. 46 pictures, each shown from its **starts at** words until the next picture's words.
